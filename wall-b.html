California corner · plan view

The front and back walls butt against Wall B's inside face, so Wall B's lining would have nothing to nail into at the corner. A second stud laid flat beside the end stud gives it a 36 wide nailing strip and something solid to screw the end wall into. The same detail is mirrored at the back-wall end.

Over the door · section

Both openings get a header made of two planks stood on edge with a 20 strip of ply between them, which makes it exactly 90 thick to match the wall. Stood on edge, two planks are far stiffer than two laid flat, which matters over the 1.8 m window. The double top plate runs the full 5035.

What changed from the room model

  • Double top plate. Two 5035 plates on top. The wall stays 2490 high, so every stud is cut 2385 instead of 2420 and the roof still sits level on the other walls.
  • California corners at both ends, on the inside face.
  • Double headers over the door and window, each resting on its own side posts (trimmers), with short studs above to carry the top plates.
  • Door is the 820 × 2030 × 40 solid door, hinged on the back-wall side and opening out into the warehouse. Opening 900 × 2080 leaves room for a 30 frame, packers, a 3 gap at the top and 12 underneath for a drop seal.
  • Window (1800 × 850 on its side) is centred between the front wall and the door, with the bottom of the frame 1300 above the floor, just above the piano.

Cut list · Wall B

Plates need 5.4 m stock; studs come out of 2.4 m lengths with almost no waste. The header packers are 20 ply strips cut to the header length × 90.

Mark-out on the plates

Measured from the front-wall end to the near edge of each upright. Clamp the two plates together and mark them both at once.
