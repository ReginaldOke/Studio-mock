Holding the door gap for the lift

  1. Lay the two bottom plank pieces in a straight line with the door gap between them at 935. A string or a straight plank along their bottom edge keeps them lined up.
  2. Lay a spare plank at least 1550 long flat on top of the bottom plank, bridging the gap with at least 300 onto each piece. Line its bottom edge up with the bottom of the wall.
  3. Screw it down with three 65–75 mm screws each side, staggered so the plank doesn't split.
  4. Check the gap is still 935, then fit the uprights and beam supports onto the bottom plank as normal.
  5. Once the wall is standing, screw the bottom plank down on both sides of the door and fix the wall's ends to the end walls. Then unscrew the plank.

Corner strip · seen from above

The end walls butt against the inside of this wall, so right at the corner the wall boards would have nothing to nail into. An extra upright laid flat beside the last one gives them a 35 wide strip to nail into, and gives you something solid to screw the end wall to. Both ends get one.

Over the door · cut through

The beam over each opening is two planks laid flat, one on top of the other, so it fills the wall's 90 thickness and stands 70 tall. Screw the pair together every 150 or so, alternating sides, so they act as one piece; that matters most over the 1.8 m window. The two planks along the top run the full 4980.

  • The studio is 55 lower overall, so it now clears the sloping warehouse ceiling at the far corner by about 100 instead of about 44.
  • The gap for the door is 935 × 2110. That leaves room for the 30 thick door frame, spacers behind it, 3 around the door, and 12 under it for a rubber seal that drops down when the door shuts. The door opens into the room, hinged on the right as seen from outside (the left from inside). It folds back against the inside of the wall towards the end wall, which has about 990 of clear wall for the 855 door, so keep that stretch free of gear and panels.
  • The window sits between the left end and the door, within 35 of the middle so the insulation gaps stay 405. The bottom of its frame is 1300 off the floor. At 137 deep it sticks out 35 into the room past the wall boards.
  • The uprights are spaced so most gaps are exactly 405 and the crosspieces are cut 405, close to your back wall (12 uprights with gaps of about 415). Like the back wall there's one row of crosspieces, stepped up and down by a plank's width so you can nail both ends. Its underside is 1160 above the bottom plank, so a standard 1160-long insulation batt fits underneath without cutting. Four full-height gaps and four under the window take whole batts. The odd ones are in red on the drawing: a 445 gap at the right end (a batt plus a 40 strip), a 60 slot under the window, and the short strips above the door and window.

What to cut · this wall

The three long planks need 5.4 m lengths. Everything else comes out of 2.4 m lengths with almost no waste, so save the offcuts for the short pieces.

Where the uprights go

Measured from the window end (the left as seen from outside) to the near edge of each upright. Lay the bottom plank and the lower top plank side by side, clamp them, and mark both at once.
